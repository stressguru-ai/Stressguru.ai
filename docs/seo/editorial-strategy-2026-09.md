# StressGuru U.S. Editorial Strategy

**Research date:** September 19, 2026

This strategy centers professional women ages 30 to 55 in the United States. It uses current audience language, workplace evidence, public health guidance, and search-result phrasing. The priority order is qualitative because no authenticated keyword-volume provider was available. It does not claim exact monthly search volume.

## Editorial Position

The reader is the hero. StressGuru serves as a practical wellness guide for moments when work pressure, sleep disruption, anxiety, low mood, caregiving, or relationship strain begin to take over the day. Each page gives a direct answer, a small next step, honest product limits, named sources, and a clear route to professional or crisis support when appropriate.

StressGuru is an iPhone and Apple Watch wellness app with eight AI wellness personas. It is not therapy, a healthcare provider, a medical device, a diagnostic tool, or crisis care. Apple Watch and HRV observations provide context and do not diagnose a condition or determine a person's emotional state.

## Claims and Safety Guardrails

- All rankings are qualitative opportunity priorities based on supplied intent fit, immediacy, breadth, and evidence. They are not claims or inferences about exact search volume.
- Describe StressGuru as an iPhone and Apple Watch wellness app, not therapy, a healthcare provider, a medical device, a diagnostic tool, crisis care, or a substitute for professional medical or mental-health care.
- Do not diagnose, screen, score, detect, rule out, prevent, treat, or cure burnout, anxiety, depression, insomnia, perimenopause, menopause, sleep disorders, trauma, or any other condition. Use conditional language such as may, can, and might be worth discussing with a qualified professional.
- Use high-functioning anxiety and impostor feelings as audience-language labels, not formal diagnoses or evidence that a user has a condition.
- Describe every persona only as an AI-guided wellness style or guide. Do not call a persona a clinician, therapist, credentialed coach, friend, human, companion with feelings, or someone with lived experience. Functional persona labels above are not claims of human identity or expertise.
- Do not claim that an exercise, app interaction, Apple Watch, heart rate, HRV, sleep observation, notification, or self-check can determine stress, burnout, emotional state, readiness, safety, cause of symptoms, or need for clinical care. Explain wearable variability, permissions, and privacy plainly.
- Make breathing and mindfulness optional. Offer stop, skip, normal-breathing, open-eye, movement, or quiet alternatives if a practice feels dizzying, uncomfortable, or more distressing; never treat persistence as a test of wellness.
- Treat work stress as partly structural. Pair micro-practices with workload, priorities, control, flexibility, manager support, EAP, and other organizational-resource options; do not imply self-care fixes an unsafe, discriminatory, abusive, or unsustainable workplace.
- Use trauma-informed, stigma-sensitive, non-shaming language. Do not pressure users to disclose health information, confront a manager or partner, quit, take leave, pursue an accommodation, or make HR, legal, financial, employment, treatment, medication, supplement, or hormone decisions. Do not promise outcomes from any workplace script.
- For persistent, severe, worsening, or function-impairing mood, anxiety, fatigue, sleep, physical, or menopausal symptoms, encourage appropriate licensed medical or mental-health support. Encourage prompt clinical assessment for heavy or atypical bleeding, breathing pauses or gasping, chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or sleepiness that makes driving unsafe.
- On content involving hopelessness, self-harm, suicide, inability to stay safe, or urgent emotional crisis, visibly direct U.S. users to call or text 988 or visit 988lifeline.org. For immediate danger or life-threatening emergencies, direct users to call 911 or local emergency services; do not route crisis content through a persona exercise first.
- Protect privacy: do not request identifiable employer, coworker, health, or crisis details unnecessarily; do not imply app conversations are privileged or invisible to third parties; and do not use sensitive wellness data for workplace, performance, insurance, or health-status judgments.
