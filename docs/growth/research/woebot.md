# Woebot research for StressGuru.ai

**As of:** October 24, 2026
**Entity assessed:** Woebot Health and its Woebot app, not Ash

## Current availability

Woebot Health is the official identity at `woebothealth.com`. Its site describes a company founded in 2017 by clinical research psychologist Dr. Alison Darcy that builds chat-based AI wellness tools and therapeutic solutions. [1] [2]

**The Woebot app is not commercially available to new individual users.** Woebot Health’s FAQ says the app was retired on June 30, 2025, new accounts cannot be created, and prior accounts cannot be accessed. [3] A second official page says its testing program ended and that Woebot is offered only by access code through a provider, employer, or other partner. [4] This conflicts with legacy referral and user pages that still describe app download and access-code enrollment. [5] [6] For competitive planning, treat Woebot as an inactive direct-to-consumer option with no verifiable current enrollment path, rather than as a live consumer-app competitor. The company website remains online, but the review did not verify an active partner program or purchasable offering.

**Ash clarification.** Ash is a separate product: its own official site is copyrighted by Slingshot AI, and Slingshot’s site identifies Ash as its flagship product. [7] [8] No official source reviewed establishes an affiliation, rebrand, or successor relationship between Ash and Woebot Health.

## Category and public content

Woebot’s historical category is a fully automated, chat-based mental-health support app. Its referral page says its conversations use elements of cognitive behavioral therapy, interpersonal psychotherapy, and dialectical behavior therapy; it describes mood tracking, reflection, gratitude journaling, and mindfulness. It also states that the adult and adolescent products were not FDA evaluated, cleared, or approved and were adjuncts rather than replacements for clinical care. [5]

The public site has relevant but dated content:

- **Work and stress:** a May 2022 interview focuses on employee reentry anxiety, workplace pressure, home-life management, and employer support. [9] A January 2023 discussion addresses burnout among healthcare workers. [10]
- **Women and family stress:** a September 2023 perinatal interview discusses stress during the transition to parenthood and mental-health support for both parents. [11]
- **Sleep:** the public material reviewed does not present a consumer sleep program. A 2023 interview mentions sleep and physical activity only as data streams a future virtual health coach could integrate. [12]

These are content observations, not claims that topics or search queries are unowned. No search-volume, ranking, keyword-difficulty, or universal product-superiority claim is made here.

## Narrow content-gap hypotheses

Each item is a hypothesis based on the reviewed, publicly indexed Woebot material. Validate with a live U.S. search-engine results-page review, a small audience interview set, and clinical review before publication. Do not imply diagnosis, treatment, or that stress is caused by menopause or caregiving.

### 1. Perimenopause-related night waking plus next-day professional stress

**Question:** “What can I do when night sweats or a 3 a.m. wake-up leave me anxious about tomorrow’s presentation?”

**Competing URL:** https://woebothealth.com/a-i-healthcare-an-interview-with-dr-eric-topol/

**Evidence:** The NIA says the menopausal transition can coincide with caring for aging parents, supporting children, and more work responsibility, while hot flashes, night sweats, and mood changes can impair sleep. [13] In the Woebot material reviewed, sleep appears in a general future-looking interview rather than a practical consumer resource for this intersection. [12]

**Opportunity:** Create a clinician-reviewed, non-diagnostic guide for U.S. professional women 40 to 55 that connects sleep basics with a next-workday coping plan: a brief bedtime wind-down, an after-waking reset, a morning workload triage, and clear thresholds for discussing persistent sleep problems or menopausal symptoms with a licensed clinician. Cite primary health sources and avoid supplement or hormone claims.

**Status:** Hypothesis. The reviewed pages suggest a narrow public-content gap, not unowned search demand or a product deficiency.

### 2. The after-hours work-to-home transition for women carrying care responsibilities

**Question:** “How do I stop replaying a difficult work conversation when I still have family care tasks to do tonight?”

**Competing URL:** https://woebothealth.com/helping-employees-manage-reentry-anxiety/

**Evidence:** Woebot’s workplace article acknowledges work relationships, uncertainty, home-life management, and anxiety, but it is a 2022 employer-facing interview about reentry. [9] Separately, AARP and the National Alliance for Caregiving report that seven in ten U.S. family caregivers are employed and that 29% are sandwich-generation caregivers. [14]

**Opportunity:** Publish a practical “20-minute transition” resource for U.S. professional women 35 to 55 who are moving from a high-pressure workday into parenting or eldercare. Keep it behavioral and specific: end-of-day thought capture, a boundary script for colleagues, a two-item care handoff, and a short decompression practice. Position it as support for daily stress, not therapy or medical care.

**Status:** Hypothesis. It is a topic-and-format gap relative to the reviewed Woebot page, not an assertion that Woebot has no similar content anywhere or that the query has no competitors.

### 3. Perimenopause, caregiving, and sleep triage without self-diagnosis

**Question:** “Is this stress, caregiving overload, or a sleep problem I should discuss with a clinician?”

**Competing URL:** https://woebothealth.com/supporting-the-mental-health-of-both-parents-during-the-perinatal-period/

**Evidence:** Woebot’s reviewed family-oriented article is perinatal and discusses stress during the transition to parenthood. [11] The NIA advises people with ongoing sleep trouble to speak to a doctor and notes cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia as an option when guided by a trained professional experienced with women in the menopausal transition. [13] Its page also explicitly situates menopausal sleep issues alongside eldercare, children, and greater work responsibilities. [13]

**Opportunity:** Develop an editorial decision aid for U.S. professional women 40 to 55, with safety-reviewed prompts that distinguish immediate self-care from making a nonurgent appointment. It should not diagnose insomnia, anxiety, depression, or menopause. Include a short symptom-and-context tracker that users can bring to a clinician, plus escalation language for urgent mental-health concerns.

**Status:** Hypothesis. The opportunity is a bounded, clinically cautious editorial format. It does not claim superiority over Woebot or any other tool.
