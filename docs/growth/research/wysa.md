# Wysa research for StressGuru.ai

*Snapshot date: October 24, 2026. Author: Manus AI.*

## Identity, category, and commercial availability

`wysa.com` is Wysa's own current website, not an Ash property. Wysa presents itself as a clinically backed, AI-driven behavioral-health platform for individuals, employers, health systems, insurers, and young people. Its individual product is an AI conversational space plus a self-care library. Its employer and health-system offerings add benefit navigation, care-pathway support, and, where included, human wellbeing coaching. [1] [2]

For a U.S. consumer, Wysa is commercially available as of this snapshot. The U.S. Apple App Store lists **Wysa: Mental Wellbeing AI** as a free iPhone app with in-app purchases; the live listing showed annual Premium purchases, guided-support purchases, and a version dated October 9. The Google Play listing was updated October 5, 2026 and also offers in-app purchases. Wysa says AI chat and tools in conversation are free, while a paid tier expands the tool library; its premium human-support service is available in select locations, including to select U.S. users by audio/video. Store prices and price labels can change, so do not reuse a headline price without rechecking the storefront. [3] [4] [5]

**Category:** This is an AI-enabled wellbeing and self-help product, not a diagnosis, therapy, or crisis service. Wysa's own intended-use statement describes self-help and self-monitoring of mental wellbeing. It says the app does not provide medical advice or diagnoses, is not recommended for crisis situations or severe and enduring mental-health problems, and does not replace qualified professional care. [4]

**Woebot availability check:** Do not treat Wysa as a substitute data point for Woebot. Woebot Health's current user page says new U.S. users must be in a study or have an access code from a provider, employer, or other partner. STAT reported on July 2, 2025 that Woebot Health had shut down its core product that week. That contrast supports describing Wysa as an actively downloadable U.S. consumer app, while avoiding a claim that either company is universally superior. [6] [7]

## Public stress, work, and sleep coverage

Wysa already has broad public coverage of this problem set. Its individual page says its 200-plus-tool library includes support for work stress, breathing, meditation, and cognitive-behavioral approaches. Its app-store description includes everyday stress, anxiety, sleep, and work conflict. For employers, Wysa explicitly frames use around stress keeping employees awake at 2 a.m., benefit navigation, self-care tools, and optional coaching. [2] [3] [8]

Wysa also has a public workplace report, based on a commissioned October 2022 survey of more than 1,000 U.S. employees. It reported that 26% of respondents suffered at work from insomnia or lack of sleep and that one in three women who were suffering did not reach out. These are company-commissioned, older findings, not a current population estimate, but they demonstrate Wysa's existing work-stress and sleep framing. [9]

Wysa's public evidence page describes clinical trials, service evaluations, and real-world studies across depression, anxiety, pain, and chronic conditions. Its broader claims about stress and sleep should be read within that self-help scope and alongside its stated clinical and crisis limitations. [10] [4]

## Narrow, evidence-linked content-gap hypotheses

These are **specificity and format hypotheses**, not claims that Wysa or the wider web has left a keyword “unowned.” I sampled Wysa's official product pages, blog navigation, and public search results. That showed clear generic work-stress and sleep coverage but did not surface a sampled Wysa destination aimed at the exact intersections below. This is insufficient to establish absence, rank, search volume, difficulty, or competitive superiority. Validate each with a manual on-site inventory, an actual U.S. SERP review, and audience interviews before production.

### 1. Perimenopause-related night waking and the next workday

**Question:** How can a U.S. professional woman, roughly 45 to 55, handle a night of hot flashes or waking without turning the next workday into a perfectionism spiral?

**Wysa comparison URL:** https://www.wysa.com/for-individuals

**Evidence:** Wysa publicly offers generic help for improving sleep and work stress. NIH says the menopausal transition usually begins between ages 45 and 55, and that trouble sleeping, mood changes, concentration difficulties, night sweats, and family responsibilities can coincide during this period. [2] [11]

**Opportunity:** Build a medically bounded, clinician-reviewed guide or interactive check-in around the moment of need: “awake at 2 a.m. before a presentation” and “the morning after poor sleep.” It could separate emotional regulation and work planning from medical care, acknowledge symptom variability, include a concise clinician-discussion prompt, and direct urgent or concerning symptoms to appropriate care. The useful distinction is the combined scenario, not a generic claim to solve menopause, sleep, or stress.

**Status:** Hypothesis. The sampled Wysa pages cover the constituent subjects broadly; validate whether an exact perimenopause-plus-workday resource already exists before publishing.

### 2. The employed sandwich-generation decompression routine

**Question:** What can a professional woman do in the 10 minutes between elder-care or child-care responsibilities and an evening work task, when she is too overloaded for a generic wellness exercise?

**Wysa comparison URL:** https://www.wysa.com/for-employers

**Evidence:** Wysa positions its employer offering around always-available help with employee stress and sleep. AARP and the National Alliance for Caregiving report that 29% of U.S. caregivers are in the sandwich generation and seven in ten family caregivers are employed. [8] [12]

**Opportunity:** Test a practical “caregiving-to-work transition” content package: a short decompression exercise, a one-screen task-triage prompt, boundary-setting scripts for manager or family conversations, and a next-step care-resource checklist. The angle should recognize both paid work and unpaid care without assuming all professional women are caregivers or that care responsibilities fall only on women.

**Status:** Hypothesis. Public Wysa materials demonstrate generic workplace and self-care coverage, but this review did not establish whether the exact employed-caregiver transition scenario has a dedicated Wysa resource.

### 3. A private script for sleep-disrupting work rumination after conflict

**Question:** How can a woman who has managed a difficult work conversation stop replaying it at bedtime without reducing the issue to “just relax”?

**Wysa comparison URL:** https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wysa-mental-wellbeing-ai/id1166585565

**Evidence:** Wysa's store listing explicitly includes tools for work conflict, anxiety, mindfulness, and sleep, so a generic topic page would not be differentiated. Wysa's workplace page also describes stress that keeps employees awake at night. [3] [8]

**Opportunity:** Test an intent-specific format rather than a broad article: a brief, non-diagnostic sequence for post-conflict rumination that moves from fact-versus-interpretation, to an optional repair or follow-up script, to a sleep-protective “park it until tomorrow” plan. Include clear escalation language for distress beyond self-help. Its differentiation would be work-conflict-to-bedtime specificity and practical language, not a superiority claim over Wysa's tools.

**Status:** Hypothesis. It needs manual review of Wysa's in-app exercises and current search results before any claim that Wysa lacks comparable functionality.
